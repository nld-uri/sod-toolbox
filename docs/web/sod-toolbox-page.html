SOD toolbox

Smooth orthogonal decomposition (SOD): the coordinates of a multichannel record that change most slowly in time, for modal identification, damping estimation, trend extraction and reduced-order models. Python and MATLAB, MIT license.

The decomposition

One real symmetric pencil, smoothest coordinates first

For a record X with time along the rows and its time derivative, SOD solves

Σvψ=μΣxψ,Σx=⟨xx⊤⟩,Σv=⟨x˙x˙⊤⟩

with the values μ ascending. Each value is the mean-square frequency of its coordinate q=XΨ. When the modal coordinates of an undamped structure are uncorrelated and in virial balance, the smooth orthogonal values 1/μ are exactly 1/ωi2 and the smooth modes are the linear normal modes.

Damping

State SOD returns each damped mode as a decaying plane

Damping biases the plain decomposition through the circulation of the response and, in a free decay, through the energy lost over the record. The same pencil on the state (x,x˙) returns each complex mode as a pair of coordinates spanning its modal plane, with μaμb=|s|4. A least-squares fit on the plane gives its damping.

Practical data

Derivatives, sensor noise, recognition and trends

Modes are recognised by their spectral peaks and planes by circularity, never by the smoothness order: a noise direction or a drift can be smoother than a weakly excited mode.

Nonlinear systems

Windowed SOD traces frequency-energy dependence

The toolbox also builds reduced-order models on smooth modes, linear normal modes, POD and MTA bases, with exact simulation, and a linear-core-plus-residual basis for nonlinear structures. It is the common code of the lab's SOD papers, now in preparation.
